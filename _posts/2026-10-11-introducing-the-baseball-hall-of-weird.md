The baseball [Hall of Weird](https://dnl-strnl.github.io/hall-of-weird/) is officially live, born October 10th, 2026. "HoW" for short, as in: ***HOW*** is that even possible? It’s a hall of fame for the oddities and extremes of baseball; numbers that sit outside the curve and tales that sound too unbelievable to be true.

You may be surprised to know that the first cycle in Major League history belongs to a [Buffalo Bison](https://www.baseball-reference.com/teams/BUF/). So does the first no-hitter ever thrown on the road, back in 1880. I'm a baseball and stat nerd from Buffalo, and those facts had been sitting in box scores for 140 years waiting for me to trip over them. There’s a small, stupid thrill in learning that the most ordinary place you know was once the site of something nobody’s done before.

Then, there are things my dad has told me during baseball games that I assumed he made up. A one-armed pitcher. A 3'7" hitter who wore jersey number ⅛. A pitcher who threw a no-hitter and lost. All true, all out of a relic called [*Strange But True Baseball Stories*](https://openlibrary.org/search?q=strange+but+true+baseball+stories):

I wanted a permanent home for a broader collection of players and incidents that started as a loose oral tradition between us: somewhere that every oddity gets verified, memorialized, and never lost again.

Like any hall of fame, you have to get inducted. The admission standard isn't greatness, it's impossibility: numbers that shouldn't exist, dug out of every box score since 1871 by **detectors**, simple scripts that scan the record book for statistical extremes. The Hall of Weird has three wings:

- **Stats** — One person, one impossible number.
- **Teams** — Whole franchise-seasons inducted together, for anomalies too big to pin on any single player.
- **Tales** — Named after Dad’s bookshelf. Documented strange-but-true stories that no stat table can hold. These aren’t detected, they’re witnessed. Then verified.

This hall is a [GitHub repository](https://github.com/dnl-strnl/hall-of-weird) that anyone can participate in. Each inductee is a single YAML file: name, era, the number, the story, etc. GitHub Pages compiles those files into the exhibit wall, which makes merging a pull request the induction ceremony. The PR lands, the site rebuilds, and a minute later a new plaque hangs in the hallowed hall of weirdness.

Anyone can nominate. Fork the repo, copy the template, fill in your find, open a PR. One inductee per PR, with referenced information on what makes them truly special; the details are in [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/dnl-strnl/hall-of-weird/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).
